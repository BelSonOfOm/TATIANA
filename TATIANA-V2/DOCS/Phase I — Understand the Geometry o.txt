1. The crucial conceptual shift

The architecture should not think:

“I have a memory structure. Which operator should I randomly apply?”

It should think:

“Given everything currently known, what configuration of my internal geometry would make the incoming information least surprising while remaining coherent and economical?”

That is almost exactly the useful part of predictive coding.

Predictive-coding models construct an objective from prediction errors and minimize it through local inference; modern formulations explicitly describe the energy as a sum of prediction-error terms, often precision-weighted.

So for your architecture:

input→prediction→error→structural change→new prediction.

That is much more interesting than simply saying “memory gets updated.”

It gives memory dynamics a reason to exist.
